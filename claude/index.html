Slow, salty, and done the old way

  1. 01

    Green peanuts, not dried

    Fresh-dug green peanuts from late summer into fall, so the shells drink in the brine. Off-season, we use raw dried peanuts soaked overnight.

  2. 02  Hours in the kettle

    Four hours or more at a low rolling boil, tasted as we go until the kernel is tender all the way through.

  3. 03  Soaked before serving

    They rest in the warm brine so every peanut is seasoned inside the shell, not just on it.

Questions people ask

Everything you want to know before you pull off the road.

Regular is just salt and water — pure, briny peanuts the way your grandparents made them. Cajun adds red pepper flakes, garlic, and a couple of other things. It builds slow; the heat doesn't hit until the second or third bite.
Fresh-dug green peanuts come in late summer through early fall. Outside that window we use raw dried peanuts soaked overnight — the flavor is a little different but they still take on the brine well. We'll tell you which we're running when you call.
